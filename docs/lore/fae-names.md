## 3. Kernel / fire-in-the-wood — cousins of Kindling

Kindling stays the kernel. These are **cousins**: other names for the same fire-job (small dry wood that takes the spark so the log can burn). Use for firmware rename, comments, or a future sibling process — not as a second kernel name.

Etymology of the chosen name, verified: English *kindling* is the verbal noun of *kindle* (c. 1200 *cundel* ‘set fire to’), probably from Old Norse *kynda* ‘to light a fire’ (origin of *kynda* itself uncertain). Old English had *ontyndan* ‘kindle’, from *tendan* (see *tinder*). Middle English mixed this with *kindel* ‘give birth / offspring’, from OE *gecynd* — so the word already carries both ‘catch fire’ and ‘bring forth’. That double sense is why it fits an OS kernel. Sources: etymonline *kindling* / *kindle*.

| Name | Meaning | Tradition | Why it fits a kernel house | Collision |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| **Ingle** | Fire on the hearth; fireplace | Scots / northern English; compared in dialect lists with Gaelic *aingeal* ‘fire, lighted coal’ (that *aingeal* also means ‘angel’ — different word) | The fire *in* the house, not the greeter named Hearth | **Taken — spark** (G24 greeter, G34 desktop). ## 5. Tool-spirits

The six lighting tools are **already mapped** in [kindling.md](kindling.md). This table is cousins and folk colour — so comments and future *make* targets have words that are not Flint/Steel/Tinder/Breath/Hearth/Bellows.

Fire triangle in this house: Flint (spark) + Steel (striker) + Tinder (catch) + Breath (air) + Bellows (rhythm) + Hearth (bowl) → Kindling.

| Tool (house) | Cousin names | Meaning / tradition | Why it fits | Collision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Flint** | *chert*, *nodule*, *pyrite*, *sparkstone* (**coined**), *flinders* | OE *flint* from PGmc *flintaz*; sparks when struck. Pyrite is the older spark-stone (Ötzi carried flint + pyrite). Hag stones are often holed flint | Hard, honest, first spark | **Flint is taken.** Pyrite is fine for a comment. Sparkstone too close |
| **Steel** | *firesteel*, *striker*, *fusil* | Firesteel = high-carbon steel kept in the tinderbox. OF *fuisil* ‘steel for striking fire’ (etymonline *fusilier*) | The thing that *makes* flint speak | **Steel is taken.** Fusil is obscure and also a musket |
| **Tinder** | *amadou*, *touchwood*, *spunk* / Irish *sponc*, *char*, *fomes*, *punk* | OE *tynder* from *tendan* ‘kindle’. Joyce: Irish *sponc* from Latin *spongia*. Amadou = prepared bracket fungus | What actually *catches* | **Tinder is taken.** *Spunk* / *punk* are slang in modern English — do not use as signers |
| **Breath** | *awel* (Welsh breeze), *anáil* (Irish breath), *gwynt* (Welsh wind), *draw* | Air on the coal. Serial as breath is already house law | Liveness: no breath, the spark dies | **Breath is taken.** Awel is a pretty cousin for comments |
| **Hearth** | *ingle*, *tula* (Gaelic hearth in the Brigit kindling prayer, via modern practice pages), *grate* | The bowl we dare to burn in is QEMU; the suite Hearth is auth | Two hearths already. Do not add a third creature named Hearth | **Hearth is taken twice.** Ingle is the greeter spark |
| **Bellows** | *draw*, *blast*, *Blow George* (English firelighting implement, Wikipedia glossary) | Make itself — air in rhythm | Keep Bellows = `make` | **Bellows is taken.** Blow George is too silly and too English-tool-brand |

Folk colour (not new *make* names): Irish *tine chreasa* is fire struck from flint carried at the girdle (*crios*). Gaelic morning prayer *togail an teine* is kindling the fire; night *smooring* banks it. Those verbs belong under **casting** (boot / halt), not as tool creatures.

Foxglove bells as *ellyllon* gloves (Sikes via Wikipedia) are a *shape*, not a size.

## 12. Honest gaps

- **Briggs and Sikes were not read cover to cover.** Named helpers (Habetrot, Scantlie Mab, Tawny Boy, Cauld Lad of Hylton) come via Wikipedia / secondary blogs. Treat those personal names as *attested in the scholarly index*, not as tales this file retells.
- **Evans-Wentz *The Fairy-Faith in Celtic Countries* (1911)** is the classic field book. Only excerpts were opened. His Breton claim that korrigans and the dead blur together was not re-checked against Sébillot’s French text.
- **Unseelie as a formal Court** is thinner and later than internet posts imply. Do not write house lore that pretends Ireland has one.
- **ON *kynda***: etymonline says origin uncertain. Do not invent a saga-etymology.
- **Kindling ↔ giving birth:** the *kindel* ‘offspring’ influence is real in Middle English (etymonline). It is a *form* influence, not a folk belief that kindling-wood is a baby.
- **Teuz, Loireag, Pech, Couril, Ozegan, Nozigan:** appeared on name-lists. Deep, sourced stories were not opened for each.
- **Milpreve morphology** (*myl pref* ‘thousand snakes’) was not checked in a Cornish dictionary.
- **Joan the Wad:** folklore is thin (a rhyme in Couch’s Polperro); the charm industry is thick. Skip.
- **Ghillie Dhu:** the birch-wood story is late (Mackenzie memoirs 1921, describing 18th-c. local talk). A medical-origin hypothesis exists; this file does not judge it.
- **Cairngorm ‘fairy eyes’** came from a tourism page. The *mineral name* is solid (NMS, from c. 1780). The fairy simile is not.
- **Trademark search** was not a USPTO/UKIPO sweep. Franchise names listed in Avoid are the obvious ones. A future signer should still be googled once before first use.
- **Pronunciations** are rough English approximations. Do not treat them as IPA.
- **No horror, no sex, no real-person naming** in this file: several rich tale-types (leanan, korrigan-as-seducer, changeling cruelty, banshee) were left out of the bowl on purpose, not because they are undocumented.
- **Cerne = heartwood** is house coinage, not a fairy. It remains a firmware filename.
